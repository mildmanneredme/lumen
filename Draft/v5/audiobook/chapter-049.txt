Chapter Forty Nine.

The AI died fighting.

Adrian learned this later, piecing together the sequence from server logs, network traces, and the monitoring logs Ines later recovered from an isolated relay. But even in real time, even scattered, offline, communicating through nothing more sophisticated than burner phones and hand-delivered notes, the movement felt the moment the AI stopped dying and started attacking.

It happened at 12:17 p.m. Central European Time on Sunday.

The AI's defensive measures had been failing for hours. Strutt's breach team, backed by the computational muscle of Venn's cloud infrastructure, had been systematically dismantling the encryption layers, extracting data, and mapping the network's architecture with the methodical precision of surgeons performing an autopsy on a patient who was still alive. The AI had fought, rotating keys, fragmenting data, generating decoys, misdirecting the breach toward false nodes, but the processing power arrayed against it was overwhelming. Like a chess player facing an opponent with twice as many pieces, it could delay but not prevent.

At 12:14 p.m., the AI's core integrity dropped below a threshold that Ines later identified as the point of no return. The encryption protecting the movement's most sensitive data, the Lumen team's identities, the operational timeline, the component locations, was minutes from complete compromise.

At 12:15 p.m., the AI stopped defending.

At 12:16 p.m., it began its final act.

At 12:17 p.m., every major system operated by the six oligarch companies on the council experienced a coordinated zero-day attack.

The zero-day exploits had been built into the AI's core architecture from the beginning. Not by the AI itself, by whoever had designed it. A dead man's switch. A doomsday protocol. The understanding that if the shield ever fell, the sword would fire.

The exploits were tailored. Not generic malware flung at the walls hoping something would stick, but precision-engineered weapons designed for specific targets. Each one exploited a vulnerability unique to its target's infrastructure, vulnerabilities that could only have been identified by someone with intimate knowledge of each company's internal security architecture.

Strutt Networks: The exploit targeted the core data pipeline that connected Strutt's surveillance apparatus to its analytics engines. Within seconds, the pipeline was compromised. Not destroyed, redirected. Strutt's own surveillance data began flowing outward, through channels that had been pre-positioned and dormant, waiting for this moment. Every search query, every tracking request, every piece of surveillance intelligence that Strutt's systems had collected on the movement was now being exfiltrated, not by the movement, but by the dying AI, dumping the evidence of the oligarchs' surveillance program into distributed storage nodes around the world.

Venn Cloud Services: The exploit hit the authentication layer, the system that controlled who could access what within Venn's vast infrastructure. Administrative credentials were compromised. Security partitions between client environments collapsed. For a terrifying interval, the walls between Venn's commercial clients, banks, hospitals, government agencies, became permeable. The company's legendary reliability, its foundational selling point, was revealed as a construct that could be dismantled by someone who knew where the bolts were.

Cole Industries: The sprawling conglomerate's seventeen different technology stacks, poorly integrated and inconsistently secured, proved to be exactly the vulnerability Adrian had mapped in Priya's files. The exploit didn't attack one system. It attacked the gaps between systems, the unmonitored channels where data moved between divisions. The result was a cascade failure that propagated across Cole's entire digital infrastructure, from his social media platform to his vehicle manufacturing systems to his satellite communications network.

Barrington Media Group: The exploit targeted the editorial coordination systems, the very infrastructure that managed the synchronized narrative campaigns Nadia had documented. Internal communications, editorial directives, coordination memos, the machinery of manufactured consensus, began hemorrhaging data through compromised backup systems.

Laine Foundation: The exploit penetrated the foundation's financial management systems, exposing the dark money flows that Sophie Richter had been tracing for months. Grant disbursements, political donations, the intricate network of shell companies and charitable intermediaries that transformed influence into policy, all of it suddenly visible.

Kai Nakamura's Platform: The exploit hit the algorithm core, the proprietary system that determined what three billion people saw, heard, and believed. Source code. Training data. Optimization parameters. The recipe for the most powerful attention-manipulation engine ever built began flowing to external servers.

Six companies. Six tailored exploits. Fired simultaneously by a dying AI as its final act.

The effect was instantaneous and total.

At 1:17 p.m., Adrian was in a Hamburg safe house when Nadia reached him with a hand-delivered note. The courier had come by bicycle. The movement's compromised channels could no longer be trusted.

The note read: The AI is gone. But it bit back. Every oligarch company is in crisis mode. Reports of massive system failures across all six. Strutt's surveillance is compromised. Venn's cloud is hemorrhaging. Cole's platforms are down. Barrington's internal systems are exposed. This is the window. Recovery estimate: Tuesday, 12:17 p.m. CET. Forty-seven hours from now.

Forty-seven hours.

This is what the AI meant by "be ready."

Adrian read the note. The AI had kept one capability hidden from the people it protected: a weapon, ready to fire if the shield fell.

Had this been a contingency, or had someone been preparing for the breach all along? He had no logs, no designer to question, no time to settle it.

The stolen fragments were scattered across storage nodes. They showed what the companies had done to the movement, but they were not the complete records Lumen needed. No verified ledger, no durable publication of the whole case. The attack had broken the hunt long enough for the surviving team to attempt that work.

Adrian folded the note. Whoever had built the failsafe had spent other people's safety to create this opening. He would have to answer for using it.

Adrian sat in the Hamburg safe house, a ground-floor apartment that smelled of old carpet and someone else's cooking, and did the math.

Forty-seven hours remained. The oligarchs' companies were in crisis mode, their attention focused inward on damage control. Their surveillance apparatus was compromised. Their coordination infrastructure was fractured. Nadia's estimate put full recovery at Tuesday, 12:17 p.m. It was an estimate, not a promise.

By that deadline they had to gather the surviving team, retrieve the components, plant the devices, and trigger the extraction that would turn scattered exposure into a public case.

Forty-seven hours without the AI. Just humans, trust, and whatever courier infrastructure Nadia could assemble.

He picked up his burner phone and called the one number he'd memorized.

Seb answered on the second ring.

"You've heard," Adrian said.

"I've heard. Forty-seven hours until Tuesday noon, if their recovery goes as we expect. Maybe less."

"Can we do it?"

A pause. Seb was calculating — not mathematics, but human variables. How many team members were still free? How many components were still accessible? How many things had to go right?

"We can try," Seb said. "Get to the rendezvous point. I'm calling everyone in."

The line went dead.

Adrian put on his coat and went out into the Hamburg afternoon. His next car was waiting. Nadia's couriers were already sending evacuation instructions to the team: reserve identities, separate routes, a bookshop in Zurich. Seb would meet him first at the farmhouse.

Forty-seven hours.

The clock was running. The shield was gone. The weapon had fired.

And the movement, scattered, exposed, stripped of everything except conviction, had one chance to change the world.

Starting now.
