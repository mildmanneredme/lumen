Chapter Sixty.

Detective James Morrow had been a cop for twenty-three years, and in that time he had learned to trust exactly two things: evidence and instinct. When they agreed, you had a case. When they disagreed, you had a problem.

On Monday morning, Detective James Morrow descended into Zurich on a flight from London. Santos was arriving separately from San Francisco. Morrow had finally asked for his partner after the liaison refused to provide the dossier's source records. He needed a second person to check what he was being told.

The file still placed Marsh at every city on the route. Morrow had stopped arguing with that part. What he wanted was the connection between a journey and a conspiracy, and every briefing treated the connection as established.

Santos had checked the dossier headers against public corporate documents. One redaction convention appeared in a contractor's compliance report, not in any of the agencies listed as the intelligence sources. Morrow had requested the original collection records. His liaison had replied with another summary.

That refusal mattered more than the elegance of the file. He had asked for something a legitimate investigation should be able to supply, and received a story instead.

The federal liaison's latest call added urgency: cyber attacks, compromised companies, a request that Marsh be detained before he could travel again. Morrow asked which imminent offense justified the timing. The liaison could not name one.

He wrote that down too.

The plane touched down. Morrow collected his bag, one carry-on, packed with the discipline of a man who had been chasing suspects across jurisdictions since before airport security became a performance art, and walked into the Zurich arrivals hall.

He had a lead. A strong one. Marsh had been traced to Switzerland through a passport scan at the German border, odd, because Morrow would have expected a fugitive to avoid border crossings. Unless Marsh wasn't running. Unless he was operating, executing something, too focused on his mission to realize he'd left a trail.

Or unless someone wanted Morrow to follow that trail to Switzerland.

Who benefits from me arresting Marsh right now?

The question stopped him cold in the middle of the arrivals hall, surrounded by travelers with roller bags and the universal expression of people who have just survived the indignity of commercial aviation.

If Marsh was part of the movement, arresting him would remove a key operative at a critical moment. The oligarchs, the tech billionaires whose companies were currently hemorrhaging from whatever cyber attack was causing Morrow's federal contacts to speak in hushed, panicked tones, would benefit enormously from having the movement's people pulled off the board.

If the oligarchs were the ones feeding Morrow evidence, then he wasn't conducting an investigation. He was being used as a weapon.

Morrow stood in the arrivals hall for a long time. A security guard gave him a look. He didn't care.

Twenty-three years of police work. Twenty-three years of following evidence wherever it led, because that was the job, that was the sacred principle, that was what separated good cops from bad ones. The evidence leads; you follow. You don't editorialize. You don't hypothesize. You let the facts build the case.

But what if the facts were manufactured? What if following the evidence didn't lead to justice but away from it? What if the most honest thing a detective could do was stop trusting the evidence and start trusting something older, less reliable, and infinitely more human?

His instinct said: Something is wrong with this case.

His evidence said: Marsh is guilty, and he's close. Go get him.

Morrow collected the keys to a gray Volkswagen Passat. He would pick Santos up at the airport, then test the latest lead himself.

He would find Marsh. He would look the man in the eye. And then he would decide which to trust: the evidence that was too perfect, or the instinct that was screaming at him to look behind the curtain.

The Swiss morning was cold and bright. The Alps glittered in the distance like the teeth of something enormous.

Morrow drove toward Zurich, and the doubt drove with him.
