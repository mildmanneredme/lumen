LUMEN v6 narration guide

Use one consistent narrator voice across the book. Preserve the author's earnest
philosophical register. Let action scenes move quickly; allow reflective passages
more space without turning every short sentence into a dramatic pause.

The audiobook folder contains the full narration transcript, individual chapter
transcripts, and a JSONL chunk manifest. These are local preparation files. No
audio has been generated. Choose the final voice through a short audition before
running the whole book.

Performance conventions

Italic internal thought: stay in the narrator's voice, with a quieter, closer
delivery. Preserve the distinction from spoken dialogue using the surrounding
attribution. Avoid a separate synthetic character voice for every thought.

Quoted messages and terminal output: make the source clear from the narration;
use a restrained, slightly flatter delivery. Filenames, key commands, and the
countdown have explicit spoken forms in the exported transcript.

Remembered dialogue: distinguish gently from present dialogue. Keep the same
character interpretation. Character voices should come from pace, attitude,
and phrasing; avoid caricatured national accents.

Scene transitions: the exported TTS text uses <short pause>. Chapter boundaries
are separate files. Add final chapter and part gaps during assembly, rather than
paying to generate long silence. Do not read formatting marks or this guide.

Gemini preparation

Target model: gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts.
Use structured speech_metadata for sustained performance directions. Gemini
3.8 treats transcript text as verbatim speech, so director's notes and speaker
labels should not be inserted as ordinary transcript text. The chunk manifest
stores guidance separately from the text. Its records are preparation data,
not complete API requests.

Chunks are limited to 700 words at paragraph boundaries. At an assumed
130 words per minute this is about 5.4 minutes before pauses, below the model's
published 16,384 audio-token serving limit at 25 tokens per second. If the
chosen delivery is unusually slow, shorten chunks further. Keep one selected
voice identifier and pronunciation policy for every request.

After synthesis, verify every chunk for omitted/repeated words, truncation,
pronunciation, unexpected spoken directions, silence, and changes of voice.
Repair individual chunks, then listen through the assembled chapters before
mastering. The cost estimate includes a separate 30 percent retake scenario;
it is an estimate, not a cap on billed usage.
