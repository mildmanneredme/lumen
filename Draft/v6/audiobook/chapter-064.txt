Chapter Sixty Four.

Earlier that Monday, 3:17 a.m. Lisbon time — thirty two hours remaining

The Alfama cache came out through a courier who knew Dona Maria and had the release instructions. Tomás was on his way from Zurich to Lisbon. He had wanted to open the floor himself. There was no time for that journey and the US plants. Nadia's network would bring the equipment to him.

An hour earlier, at 2:17 a.m., Dona Maria had opened the green basement door for Yannis's driver. The streets above the Tagus were quiet, the fado houses closing, the alleys empty except for the last people walking home.

The driver knew the agreed phrase. "I've come for the plants."

"They're where he left them," Dona Maria said.

There were no plants. She took him downstairs and showed him the three loose flagstones behind the workbench. Tomás had sent a drawing with the release instructions. The driver matched it to the floor, lifted the stones, and set the sealed cases on the bench.

Six cases. Black, hard-sided, each one the size of a laptop bag. The seals were intact. He checked their numbers against the list, then loaded them and Tomás's smaller field kit into the van.

At 3:17 a.m., Tomás reached Lisbon airport under the name in his reserve passport. The courier found him outside arrivals and transferred the two bags. Dona Maria had added a paper packet of pasteis de nata.

Tomás thanked the driver, then took the bags to the private room Nadia's travel contact had arranged near the terminal. He broke the storage seals and worked through his checks: charge, loopback test, tunnel software, connectors. Six devices. All operational.

He separated the cases. Three would travel with him to Austin, Menlo Park, and Seattle. The others had their own couriers and operatives already in place near the targets. Each operator had received Tomás's installation instructions months before. Today they would receive the tested device and the final payload.

His next leg was a direct reserved charter from Lisbon to Austin, departing an hour after he landed. The overnight crossing would use most of the morning in Europe. He would verify the access material and rehearse each plant in the air.

Austin first: a facilities visit during business hours. Cole's contractors had an approved maintenance window for the damaged campus systems. Tomás had the paperwork and the access data.

Menlo Park second: the delivery dock and a device lowered through an access panel beside the cable trays.

Seattle last. Venn's campus was the hardest. He had helped design its access controls, but his plan needed the emergency repairs to keep the facilities staff moving. If their normal procedures returned first, he would have to stop.

He thought of Ana, his sister. Her reporting had traced Venn's Cambodian subsidiary through the companies that insulated its owners. The 2019 police report called her death a robbery. He still carried copies of the testimony that identified the subsidiary's private military contractor.

He put the photographs away. Checked his tools. Sent the relay a brief update: "Six packages inspected. All operational. First delivery: Austin."

Then he boarded. There was work he could do in the air.

Outside the terminal, the Tagus was still black. In the cabin, Tomás opened the maintenance packet and began reading.

He kept the custard tarts beside him. A gift from someone who knew enough to wish him home, and little enough to stay safe if he did not return.

But the custard tarts were excellent.
