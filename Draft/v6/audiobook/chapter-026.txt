Chapter Twenty Six.

That evening, the briefing took place in a room that didn't officially exist.

It was beneath a dry cleaner in the Richmond District, a neighborhood that tourists never visited and that San Franciscans treated as a quiet annex to the louder parts of the city. The dry cleaner's owner, an elderly Korean woman named Mrs. Park, ran a legitimate business upstairs and asked no questions about the reinforced basement her nephew had constructed two years ago. Adrian would later learn that Mrs. Park's nephew was a structural engineer who had designed the basement to be electromagnetically shielded, a Faraday cage built into the foundation, invisible from the outside, impenetrable to signals in or out.

Seb sat across from Adrian at a card table. Between them lay a physical map of the western world: paper, printed on a commercial plotter, covered in handwritten annotations in at least three different scripts. No digital copies. No backup. If this room burned, this map burned with it.

Seb placed Priya's papers beside the map. "You've met the roles. Tonight we deal with the joins. What goes into each component, what comes out, and where a mistake in your maps can stop the next piece working."

He put a red marker on Berlin. "The Scaffold needs a manifest of everything released: source, destination, a cryptographic hash for each file. It replicates the data and the manifests across its nodes. Once they're out, we can't silently alter them."

"No correction after publication?"

"A correction would have to be published separately. The original remains."

Adrian looked at the diagrams. "Then mistakes travel as reliably as the evidence."

"Yes. Ines will make sure you understand that. There are thousands of nodes in forty-seven countries. Their operators consented to distributed computing. They haven't been told what their machines will publish."

"That's not informed consent."

"No," Seb said. "It's one of the costs. I want you to see it before you agree to carry it."

The next marker went on Tallinn. "Andrei opens the perimeter. He needs the current authentication boundaries and service routes. A map that's right about where a database sits but wrong about which credentials reach it is useless to him."

"Six targets?"

"Strutt's social platforms. Cole's companies. Venn's cloud. Barrington's media network. Laine's foundation systems. NovaTok."

Kai's company. Adrian had already seen its name on the target list. Hearing it spoken beside the others still made him look down at the table.

Seb did not press him. He turned to the Silicon Valley marker and spread out Priya's diagrams. Four companies were fully mapped. Laine's systems and NovaTok were not. Adrian recognized the two incomplete sketches from Sunday's meeting.

His hands did not want to hold the pages. Three nights ago they had gathered to discuss her death. Now he was reading her notes, inheriting the work she had asked to share with him. The nausea stirred in his gut. He breathed through it and kept reading.

"She had people inside the other companies," Seb said. "They helped with the architecture, usually without knowing its purpose. The contacts are in her encrypted working files. You decide which ones can safely be approached again."

"And the gaps I can't verify?"

"Mark them. Don't turn an inference into a fact because we need it to be one."

Geneva. "Sophie's tools have to find the financial records and preserve the path back to their source. Your maps give her the locations. Her analysis connects a payment to a company, a recipient, a decision."

"And avoids confusing money passing through an account with money belonging to it."

"Exactly. Ask her what your diagrams don't tell her."

Lisbon. "Tomás installs the hardware. He needs the physical access points that reach the services you've mapped. A device on the wrong side of a security boundary is a device we can't use."

"All of them in place before activation."

"Installed, checked, armed. No one waits for trigger day to walk through a lobby."

Reykjavik. "Ólafur gives those devices their common schedule. Signals take different amounts of time to reach different places. He measures those delays and sends each command early enough that the arrivals fall inside the same four-hundred-millisecond spread."

"And completion still depends on the target."

"Seconds inside each system. All six start before any company can warn another and impose an effective lockdown. Our measured minimum for that cross-target response is five seconds. Ólafur's schedule buys us the lead; your maps keep a local operation from wasting it."

Adrian looked at the red markers. For the first time the six roles felt less like a list than a chain of things that could fail.

Seb capped the marker and sat back.

"When you meet them," Seb said, "ask what they need from the next person. The work is separate. The failure won't be."

"And my job is to be the connective tissue. The maps that make the parts into a whole."

"Your job is to finish what Priya began. As much of it as you can verify." Seb's voice softened. "You'll fly to Tallinn first. Then Berlin, Geneva, Lisbon, and Reykjavik. At each stop, you'll meet the team member, review their component, understand how it interfaces with the maps, and identify what's missing. Your travel documents are ready. Your cover identity is established. You leave in fourteen hours."

Adrian looked at the map spread across the card table. Six red dots on a paper world. Lines connecting them in a web that, if it held, would change everything.

"One question," he said.

"Go."

"Priya had notes, working files, drafts, communications with her sources. Where are they?"

Seb hesitated. "The police returned her personal devices to her family. We can't approach them without exposing what she was doing. But Priya was careful. She kept her operational files separate from her personal life, on an encrypted partition of a device she never took to work. The device is in a safe location."

"A safe location?"

"Hidden. Physically hidden. The retrieval team recovered it on Thursday. The files are being checked tonight. You'll have verified copies before you leave: her methodology, her contacts, the unfinished work. From there, you build."

Adrian nodded. He looked at the map one final time.

Six cities. Five strangers. A dead woman's unfinished work.

And somewhere out there, an assassin who had already proven she could reach anyone.

"Fourteen hours," Adrian said. "I'd better pack."

"Travel light," Seb replied. "You won't be coming home for a while."
